Inside the engine

Everything a Go engine needs. Nothing it doesn't.

One binary plays, analyzes, referees, generates training data, and gates its own successors. The design choices are written down — every major one has an ADR.

Rules that hold up

Chinese area scoring and Tromp-Taylor, positional superko, suicide ban, SGF import and export. Scoring symmetry and arena-bias invariants are tested, not assumed.

chinese_scoring_test.go · score_symmetry_test.go

PUCT search

Monte-Carlo tree search with policy priors, a transposition table, tree reuse between moves, root-parallel playouts — plus forced root playouts and policy-target pruning from the KataGo paper.

AdvanceTree · playout-cap randomization

Two brains

A hand-tuned heuristic evaluator — stones, liberties, territory, move priors — and a 6-block residual net running through in-process ONNX Runtime at ~0.9 ms per position. HTTP sidecar as fallback.

-eval heuristic|onnx · 1.2M params · 4.6 MB

Speaks GTP

Drop it into Sabaki or Lizzie as an engine. Think-time or fixed-playout budgets, time_left handling, and an SGF of the game written on quit.

-gtp -size 9 -think-time 5s -o game.sgf

A data factory

Self-play exports JSONL rows the trainer consumes directly: visit-count policy targets, opponent policy, ownership labels, 8 spatial feature planes and 4 globals per position.

policy · policy_opp · ownership · full_search

A merciless arena

Champion versus challenger under identical configs. Wilson confidence intervals, early rejection when the gate is mathematically out of reach, and a config hash stamped on every report.

promote only if win rate clears 0.55

The training loop

Nothing gets promoted without a fight.

Every cycle: the champion plays itself, a network trains on the games, and the result walks into an arena against the very champion that generated its data. Lose, and it's discarded. Win past the Wilson gate, and it takes the throne — after the old champion is archived.

The gate

A challenger needs a win rate whose Wilson interval clears 0.55 over up to 200 equal-config games — minimum 100 before promotion, early rejection the moment the bound becomes unreachable.

The proof

Training runs in PyTorch; play runs in Go. A parity harness holds them together: 1,721 positions, bit-exact between Go ONNX Runtime and the Python reference session.

The honest part

The net is young. The current champion took the title in cycle 3; challengers since have peaked at a 30.5% arena win rate. The loop is the point — the strength is earned, cycle by cycle, on a 2 GiB cloud box.

Hot path discipline

      
The board never allocates.

      
MCTS lives and dies by the inner loop. Gofer mutates one board and undoes, instead of cloning per playout — a measured decision (undo: 23 ns, clone: 130 ns), recorded in ADR 0002. Legal-move generation dropped from 1,158 heap allocations to 7 with reusable scratch buffers.

      
And it stays that way: make bench-check compares every run against a committed baseline, in CI, on every push.
